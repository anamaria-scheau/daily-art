## Description

A man stands on a rocky peak with his back to us, looking out over a sea of fog.

## Story

A man stands on a rock, back to us, coat stirring in the wind, a walking stick in his hand. Below him, fog drowns a landscape of peaks and valleys. He is not hurrying anywhere. He has simply stopped at the top, and he looks.

What is he thinking? Triumph at reaching the summit? A feeling of being tiny in front of something enormous? Loneliness? Friedrich gives no answer, because we cannot see his face. That is the trick: the figure is an empty seat, and you sit in it. Whatever you feel, awe, calm, a little fear, becomes his feeling too.

Even the view is a daydream. Friedrich is thought to have built it in his studio from separate sketches of different real places in the mountains of Saxony and Bohemia. The famous landscape never existed as such. It is a landscape of the mind, which is exactly what the Romantics wanted to paint. Nobody knows who the wanderer is, and some people think he is a stand-in for the painter himself.

## Look closer

- The man is seen from behind, so you stand in his place and share his view.
- The fog blurs the distance, so the world seems to go on forever.
- The rocks in front are sharply painted, while the faraway peaks almost melt into the sky.

## Context

Painted around 1818 in Dresden, only a few years after the end of the Napoleonic wars. Germany was divided into many small states, and many people dreamed of freedom and unity. Some historians suggest that the wanderer's dark coat resembles the clothing worn by young German patriots of the time, which may give the picture a political shade as well as a spiritual one.

Romanticism valued emotion, imagination and the overwhelming power of nature, the 'sublime', over reason and order. Friedrich's landscapes often feel like silent prayers. This painting is now in the Hamburger Kunsthalle in Germany and has become an icon of the whole movement.

## Artist

Caspar David Friedrich (1774–1840) was born in Greifswald, on the Baltic coast, studied in Copenhagen and settled in Dresden. He painted silence: fog, ruins, moonlit seas and lonely figures. He was admired in his time, but later fell out of fashion, and after a stroke in 1835 he painted very little. He was rediscovered in the early twentieth century.

## When this was painted

In 1818 Friedrich was in his mid-forties and had just married Caroline Bommer. The couple travelled to the island of Rügen on their honeymoon, and he painted the famous Chalk Cliffs on Rügen that same year. It was a happy and productive period for an artist who is usually remembered for solitude.
