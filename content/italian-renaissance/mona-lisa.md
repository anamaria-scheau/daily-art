## Description

A woman sits calmly, hands folded, with a faint smile that seems to change the longer you look.

## Story

She looks at you, and the longer you look, the less sure you are what she feels. Amused? Patient? Hiding a secret? Leonardo built that uncertainty on purpose. The corners of her mouth and eyes are blurred into soft shadow, so your own mind fills in the emotion, and it seems to shift every time you glance back.

Think of what she might be thinking. She had to hold this pose for hours, session after session. Giorgio Vasari, writing decades later, claimed that Leonardo hired musicians and jesters to keep his sitter relaxed. If that is true, her famous smile might simply be a person trying not to laugh at a juggler.

And then there is the theft. In 1911 Vincenzo Peruggia, a former Louvre worker, hid in the museum and walked out with her under his coat. For two years she was gone, until he tried to sell her in Florence. Overnight a quiet portrait became the most famous face on Earth. Today you stand in a crowd behind a barrier, and she still calmly looks back.

## Look closer

- The corners of her mouth and eyes are painted in soft, smoky shadows, with no hard outlines.
- The winding landscape behind her looks slightly different on the left and on the right.
- Her hands are relaxed and layered, a pose that was fresh and modern in her time.

## Context

The painting was begun in Florence around 1503. The sitter is usually thought to be Lisa Gherardini, wife of a silk merchant named Francesco del Giocondo, which is why it is called La Gioconda in Italian and La Joconde in French. Leonardo kept working on it for years and never handed it over to the client. He took it with him to France, and it ended up in the collection of the French kings, and later in the Louvre.

Leonardo used a technique called sfumato, from the Italian word for 'smoky': many extremely thin glazes of paint that melt light into shadow with no visible edges. The portrait also shows the Renaissance fascination with the individual: not a saint or a king, but a person, with a personality you can almost feel.

## Artist

Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519) was born in Vinci, near Florence. He was a painter, engineer, anatomist and endless note-taker, and he filled thousands of pages of notebooks, written in mirror writing, with flying machines, water studies and dissections. Fewer than twenty paintings are firmly attributed to him, because he was always starting new ideas and leaving things unfinished.

## When this was painted

Around 1503 Leonardo was about fifty and back in Florence after years in Milan and a stint as a military engineer for Cesare Borgia. That same period he was working on a huge battle mural for the city hall, in rivalry with a young Michelangelo. The portrait of Lisa was a quieter project that he kept coming back to and refining.
