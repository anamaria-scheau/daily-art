## Description

A glowing night sky swirls above a sleeping village, with a dark cypress tree reaching up like a flame.

## Story

Imagine the sky as something alive. It swirls, glows and breathes. Stars burst like fireworks, each inside its own halo, and the moon is a fiery crescent. Below, a tiny village sleeps with a few lit windows, and a dark cypress climbs toward the sky like a flame turned black.

Van Gogh painted this while staying at an asylum in Saint-Rémy, at a fragile time of his life. From his window he could see the sky before dawn and the hills, but he painted the picture later, by day, from memory, and he invented the village, giving it a church spire that looks more like those of his native Netherlands than of Provence. So it is not a view. It is a feeling about a night, remembered and remade.

What might he have felt? Some people see turmoil in the sky, others comfort: the village is calm while the heavens are wild, as if the energy of the universe and the quiet of human life exist side by side. The viewer feels both, small and held at once. Van Gogh once wrote to his brother Theo that just as we take a train to reach a town, perhaps death is how we reach a star.

## Look closer

- Follow the swirling strokes in the sky: they look like waves, wind and light at once.
- The tall dark cypress connects the earth to the sky.
- Count the stars: each one is surrounded by a halo of tiny, separate brushstrokes.

## Context

The painting dates from June 1889 and was made at the asylum of Saint-Paul-de-Mausole in Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, in the south of France. Van Gogh had checked himself in voluntarily after his breakdown in Arles the winter before. He was allowed to paint, and the staff gave him a room to use as a studio.

Post-Impressionism came after Impressionism's quick studies of light. Artists like Van Gogh, Cézanne and Gauguin used color, line and rhythm to express feeling and structure. Van Gogh's thick paint and swirling strokes are among the most recognizable results. The painting has been in the Museum of Modern Art in New York since 1941.

## Artist

Vincent van Gogh (1853–1890) was born in Zundert, in the Netherlands. He worked as an art dealer, a teacher and a preacher before turning to painting in his late twenties. In under ten years he made around 2,100 artworks, including about 860 oil paintings, and he died at 37 in Auvers-sur-Oise. His brother Theo supported him and kept hundreds of his letters.

## When this was painted

In 1889 Van Gogh was 36 and recovering in the asylum after his crisis in Arles, supported financially by Theo. That same year he painted many of his most famous works: cypresses, olive trees and wheat fields. His letters suggest that painting was the thing that kept him steady.
